When a business decides it needs software, one of the first questions is whether to buy an existing product or build something custom. Both are valid choices. The mistake is choosing before you understand the problem.

## What off-the-shelf software does well

Off-the-shelf products — accounting packages, point-of-sale systems, email tools, general CRMs — are built for common needs shared by many businesses.

- **Faster to start.** You can often be up and running within days.
- **Lower upfront cost.** The development cost is shared across many customers.
- **Proven.** Popular products have been tested by many users.

If your process is fairly standard — basic bookkeeping, for example — an existing product is often the sensible choice.

## Where off-the-shelf software falls short

The limits appear when your business doesn't work the way the product assumes it does.

- **You adapt to the software,** instead of the software adapting to you. Teams build workarounds, and workarounds become new manual processes.
- **Disconnected tools.** Businesses end up with one tool for sales, another for stock and a spreadsheet to connect them.
- **Ongoing licence costs** grow with your team, and features you need may never be added.
- **Local realities** — mobile-money payments, intermittent connectivity, local reporting needs — may not be considered.

## What custom software does well

Custom software is designed around your workflows, requirements and goals.

- **It fits the way you operate,** including the processes that make your business different.
- **It can connect your operations** in one system, or integrate with tools you already use.
- **It grows with you.** Features can be added as the business evolves.
- **You control it** — the roadmap, the data and the priorities.

The trade-off is a higher upfront investment and the need for a capable, reliable development partner.

## How to decide

Ask a few practical questions:

1. **How standard is the process?** The more unique it is to your business, the stronger the case for custom.
2. **How many tools are you juggling?** If staff move data between several systems every day, an integrated solution may pay for itself.
3. **Where is the business going?** Choose software for where you'll be in three years, not just today.
4. **What's the cost of the workarounds?** Count the hours and the errors, not just the licence fee.

Sometimes the answer is a combination: off-the-shelf tools for standard functions, with custom software for the core of what makes your business work.
